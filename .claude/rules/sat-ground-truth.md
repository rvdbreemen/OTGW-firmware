## SAT ground truth: our implementation is leading (maintainer directive, 2026-11-21; replaces the 2026-10-22 ranking)

SAT (Smart Autotune Thermostat) was designed on paper by Sergeant D (George) and first turned into code by Alex Wijn (Alexwijn) as a Python Home Assistant component. Our firmware started as a port of that code at commit `7beaaf531ee99dc74a19ac74f3c06ce7f2922cfb` (2026-03-18, then branch `releases/thermo-nova`). Since then Sergeant D and Robert have recorded hundreds of decisions and new rules that go beyond it. Robert, 2026-11-21: "De implementatie die wij hebben van SAT is leidend. En alle keuzes die George heeft gemaakt en ik heb gemaakt, zijn tezamen de SAT-implementatie." Alex's code "is niet langer de leidende implementatie, maar goede referentie en we willen uitgelijnd blijven met deze implementatie": we borrow from it when needed, learn from it and refer to it. Sergeant D stays the SAT designer and decides SAT behaviour.

**Ground truth, in this order:**
1. Sergeant D's recorded corrections and decisions, and Robert's SAT decisions, in `docs/sat/decisions/sergeant-d-corrections.md` (one numbered entry each, with date and Discord link).
   The register is private since 2026-11-21 (maintainer directive): it lives in the private repo rvdbreemen/OTGW-sat-decisions, mounted as the submodule `docs/sat/decisions/`; public files, commits, ADRs and Discord posts refer to entries by number and never quote them.
2. Our SAT implementation: `src/OTGW-firmware/SAT*.ino`, `SATtypes.h`, the SAT ADRs in `docs/adr/` and `docs/sat/firmware-sat-spec.md`. Where the code and a recorded decision disagree, the decision wins and the code is a bug.
3. The OpenTherm specification and the OpenTherm application notes (`docs/opentherm specification/`, `other-projects/OT-Spec/`), and field data from testers (captures, long-run recordings).
4. Reference, not leading: Alex Wijn's SAT at its latest 5.x release (maintainer directive 2026-11-28: "From now on always compare to latest SAT component from Alexwijm 5.x."), `https://github.com/Alexwijn/SAT/releases`, local snapshot `other-projects/SAT-develop/` at the commit in its `UPSTREAM_COMMIT` (release 5.1.0 = 9c2fad9, 2026-11-25; the folder name is historical), and `docs/sat/thermo-nova-spec.md` (written from 7beaaf5). Comparisons with Alex's code use that release. Older citations at 7beaaf5 or at develop ba515c8 stay valid; check them with `git -C ../OTGW-reference/SAT-git show <commit>:custom_components/sat/<file>` (full upstream clone next to the repo).

**Rules for every statement about SAT behaviour** (in a plan, task note, ADR, spec, Discord post or review):
- Always factual. Cite the source as `file:line` (our firmware, or `custom_components/sat/<file>:<line>` at a named commit of Alex's code) and open that location in the current session before citing it. No citation, no claim.
- Never fill a gap with plausible behaviour. If neither the code nor a recorded decision shows it, write "not found in the code" and ask Sergeant D.
- Describe behaviour in time order from the code (what happens first, what triggers the next step, which timers run). The start-up sequence and the suppression mechanism are the known hallucination hot spots: for those, trace every path and write a timeline.
- **When Sergeant D refers to thermo-nova or to Alex Wijn's SAT, treat it as a reason to look at that code, not as ground truth.** Compare our implementation with his: give Sergeant D the factual differences, the pros and cons of each, and a recommendation how to proceed. Base the comparison on every relevant recorded decision in `docs/sat/decisions/sergeant-d-corrections.md`, on our own rules, on the OpenTherm specification and the OpenTherm Application Functional Specification v1.1f (`other-projects/OT-Spec/`, the low-load application note Robert means, 2026-11-21), and where useful on internet research and simulations. The comparison follows the evidence both ways: it may conclude that Alex's way is better on a point, and then we borrow it through a normal decision.
- **Field data validates our implementation.** Use tester captures to check that our SAT does what the recorded decisions say, and replay the same data through both implementations to learn how each behaves.
- **Every correction or decision by Sergeant D is recorded in `docs/sat/decisions/sergeant-d-corrections.md`, in the same session and before the change it leads to is implemented** (maintainer directive, 2026-10-23). This covers corrections of our claims or code, answers to our questions, and choices between options, also when they arrive as a one-line reply on Discord. One numbered entry per item: date, his words quoted verbatim, the Discord message link, what it settles compared with our firmware and, where relevant, Alex's code (`file:line`), the task it drives, and what stays open. When a reply is short or ambiguous, record the reading you act on as an interpretation and say so. A later entry that changes an earlier one names the earlier entry; old entries are never rewritten. After adding an entry, refresh the provenance index with `python scripts/sat_corrections_index.py --update` (TASK-1630).
- **Every SAT decision is checked against Sergeant D's recorded decisions before it is taken** (maintainer directive, 2026-10-23). Read `docs/sat/decisions/sergeant-d-corrections.md` first. A plan, review, ADR, spec change or Discord answer about SAT names the entries it relies on and says, per relevant entry, whether it agrees. A conflict with an entry is never resolved silently: report it with the entry number and the citations, and put it to Sergeant D. A reviewer checks the same list and treats an unmentioned conflict as a finding.
- **Unclear SAT behaviour is resolved by questioning Sergeant D step by step, on facts** (maintainer directives, 2026-10-24 and 2026-11-21): discuss with him on the basis of the code, our rules, the specification and field data. Ask one question per Discord message, in plain English (he is a Greek native speaker), with the possible answers (A/B/C, each with its consequence and its citation) and our recommendation of the best one, and wait for his answer in the chat before the next question. Pace the questions to his presence (maintainer directive, 2026-10-25): while he is active (he just answered a question), post the next queued question at once, however little time has passed. Check the chat every hour. If he does not react within the first hour after a question, wait quietly: no post while he is away. As soon as he is back (any new message from him in the channel), ask the first open question again, short, with a link to the original; he may simply have forgotten it. Keep the queue and each question's status in `docs/sat/decisions/open-questions.md` (status words queued, asked, answered, dropped; refresh its Live queue with `python scripts/sat_open_questions.py --update`, TASK-1629); record every answer in `docs/sat/decisions/sergeant-d-corrections.md` before acting on it, and turn each decided answer into a backlog task that implements it. Take the time: an open question blocks the implementation, never a guess.
- Before changing how SAT controls the boiler, the plan quotes our current implementation of that area and the recorded decisions it rests on, names how Alex's code handles it where that helps (with citations), and waits for Sergeant D's OK (see `.claude/skills/implement-next-task/SKILL.md`, step 4). Arguments from memory do not count.
- A claimed difference between Alex's code and our firmware is published only after a second reader has opened both citations and tried to refute it.
- Refresh `other-projects/SAT-develop/` only on purpose, when a newer 5.x release is published: one commit that records the new release tag and commit in its `UPSTREAM_COMMIT` and in the other-projects README, and says what changed. Never update the local copy silently.
